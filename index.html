Why Retaining Walls Lean

  • No drainage: water trapped behind a wall builds pressure until the wall bows or topples. Gravel backfill and a drain pipe are not optional extras.
  • Too thin at the base: a wall that is the same thickness top to bottom fights soil pressure with the wrong shape. A wider base and a proper footing change the leverage completely.
  • Built on topsoil: like footings, walls need firm, undisturbed ground or compacted fill at the base — not garden soil that will settle.
  • Too tall for its design: small garden walls under about 3 to 4 feet are a DIY-scale job; taller walls, surcharges from driveways above, and tiered walls need engineering.

Steps, Worked Through

Take 6 steps, 4 feet wide, with a 7-inch rise and an 11-inch run. Each step, treated as a solid block, is 4 ft wide × (7/12) ft high × (11/12) ft deep = 2.14 cubic feet. Six steps give 12.83 cubic feet, or 0.48 cubic yards neat. With a 10% buffer, plan for 0.52 yards — about 24 eighty-pound bags. That small total is exactly why steps are often a bag job, while the wall behind them may not be.

Comfort matters as much as volume: a rise around 7 inches and a run around 11 inches feels natural to climb. A rise over 8 inches or a run under 10 inches will feel steep every single day, no matter how perfect the concrete is.

Retaining Wall Volume, in One Line

For a straight wall, volume is length × height × thickness, all in feet, divided by 27 for cubic yards. A 20-foot wall, 3 feet high and 12 inches thick is 20 × 3 × 1 = 60 cubic feet, or 2.22 yards neat — before footing, drainage gravel, and waste. Always price the footing separately; it is often as much concrete as the wall itself.

Steps & Walls Questions

How do I keep steps from cracking at the nosing?

Keep the nosing thickness generous, cure properly, avoid de-icing salts in the first winter, and do not add water to the mix on site to make finishing easier — it weakens exactly the edge that takes the most wear.

Do I need a footing under a small retaining wall?

Yes, in most cases: a compacted gravel base at minimum, and a concrete footing for walls over a couple of feet, on soft ground, or carrying a load above. A wall sitting directly on topsoil will settle unevenly.

What drainage does a retaining wall need?

Free-draining gravel behind the wall, a perforated drain pipe at the base discharging to daylight, and weep holes or gaps so water never builds up pressure behind the face.

Can I pour steps in sections?

Yes, with construction joints planned in advance and reinforcement continuity where the design requires it. Pouring one step at a time without a plan leaves weak, visible joints in the middle of the flight.
